$ man sphere-shade

Two spheres, light given. Draw the terminator across the sphere in one stroke — the drill fits the axis of your line — then drag the three dots: core shadow, bounce (reflected) light, occlusion shadow. Hit done. Keys 1–4 pick a mark (1 is the terminator), arrows nudge it, enter is done. Press done before you have drawn anything and it asks first, so a stray press never costs you a sphere.

Scored on placement only, with a 3° grace on every angle — this is shading anatomy, not rendering. On reveal your ink sits next to the lilac answer with a dashed line from each mark to where it belonged, and a flat-value plan sphere shows the whole anatomy named. The ticks read ✓ for 75+, ~ for 45+, ✗ below, followed by how far off you were and which way to turn.

The light is a real 3D vector: the sun on the sheet gives its direction, the little plan view top-right gives its depth — coming toward you, or from behind the sphere. That depth is what fattens the terminator ellipse, swings the core off centre and stretches the cast shadow.

Studio tip: the terminator is geometry, not mood — it is the great circle 90° from the light, so its axis never depends on the tilt. Find it first and everything else hangs off it.
